# The US data centers most stranded by permitting risk

_Screened 1814 of 1824 OSM-mapped US data-center buildings on 2026-08-08, rolled up to **1154 campuses**. Physical viability from Mireye; permitting risk from the Moratorium Nation inventory. Every value cited; method reproducible._

## What we found

- **500** campuses are DISPUTED — physically viable but sitting inside the blast radius of an active moratorium.
- **113** are FLAGGED — a named risk to resolve early.
- **541** clear a first-pass screen clean.

**Stranded viability** is the headline metric: physical site quality minus permitting risk. A high score means an excellent site the market is being forced to walk away from — exactly the parcels where a community-benefit package (grid flexibility, waste-heat reuse, water offsets) would unlock the most value.

## Method

1. Pull all US data centers tagged in OpenStreetMap (1824 buildings).
2. Screen each on 25 cited Mireye fields (terrain, power, hazard, externalities) plus nearby active moratoria.
3. Roll buildings up to campuses (operator + spatial clustering).
4. Rank by stranded viability = physical mean − permitting score.
5. Every screened site emits a content-hashed attestation; nothing is asserted without a citation and a re-checkable source.

_Not construction advice. Proximity is not deliverability; a screen tells you where to look, not what you'll find on the ground. Campus rollup is operator + 4 km clustering; a bare-branded cluster in one metro may span more than one physical site._
